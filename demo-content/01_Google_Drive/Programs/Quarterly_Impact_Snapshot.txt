## What this snapshot contains
These figures exist only to demonstrate how the pantry can bring operational evidence into grant reporting and organizational memory. They are not the actual results of a nonprofit, and should not appear as customer traction or verified community impact in the video.

The fictional outcomes register contains 1,260 household visits for the quarter. This counts visits rather than unique households, so a household visiting more than once contributes more than one visit. The register shows 401 July visits, 419 August visits and 440 September visits. The source of record for an actual organization would be its approved program database.

## How visits were counted
Staff complete a tally after each distribution. They do not record household names in public-facing outcome summaries. Volunteers can help with traffic flow but are not asked to access private case files. Sam consolidates weekly totals, checks unusual days, and signs off monthly counts.

## Fictional monthly trends
July: 401 visits. August: 419 visits. September: 440 visits. The trend is mildly upward, but three months of samples do not demonstrate a real change in community need. Any explanation should be sourced to staff notes and external conditions rather than invented.

## Why definitions matter
One visit is one household receiving groceries in a distribution session. A volunteer shift is one completed attendance record, not necessarily a distinct volunteer. Food collected and food distributed are different measures and should not be merged. An estimated meal equivalent should be labeled with its conversion method before being used externally.

## Review before publication
Sam checks whether any event days were missed. Finance reconciles relevant spending before a grant report is prepared. The Executive Director authorizes the final story and any public statistic. A draft created by AI does not replace this review.
