# Recording and editing guide

## Capture

- Default timeline: 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 30 fps unless submission rules say otherwise. Keep the same frame rate through the edit.
- Capture at native high resolution, ideally enough for a modest crop into a 1080p timeline. Confirm UI text remains readable in the exported result.
- Use a clean browser window, consistent dimensions, the real product theme and synthetic demo content. Close unrelated tabs and enable Do Not Disturb yourself.
- Record each action as a separate 8–20 second take. Leave two seconds of stillness before and after; move the cursor deliberately and avoid circling.
- Capture clean result stills in addition to video. They are useful for a reading hold, but should not imply an interaction happened that did not.
- Record visuals separately from final narration. This makes last-minute website changes easy to replace.
- With Screen Studio, make cursor and zoom decisions there, then export selected clips into the finishing editor. Avoid applying a second overlapping zoom in CapCut.

## The whoosh → pause → click → result sequence

Use this for a major action, such as generating the playbook. The exact click is time zero:

| Relative time | Picture | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| −0.45 to −0.10 s | Ease from overview to about 135–150% on the target; cursor arrives | Short soft whoosh rises with the move |
| −0.10 to 0.00 s | Camera settles; button readable | End the whoosh; leave a tiny gap |
| 0.00 s | Actual click; optional subtle ring begins | Quiet, dry click exactly on contact |
| 0.00 to +0.25 s | Ring expands and fades; retain actual UI response | Keep space around the click |
| On result appearance | Reframe if needed, then hold for 1.5–3 seconds or more | Small confirmation tone only if useful |

At 30 fps, a three-frame pause is 0.10 seconds and a nine-frame ring is 0.30 seconds. Use these as starting points, not rigid rules. The result tone should follow the actual visible result, not an arbitrary delay.

For a manual zoom in CapCut: select the clip, add a scale/position keyframe, advance about 12 frames, add the closer view, and ease the motion if the editor exposes easing. Position the button in a comfortable reading area. Hold, then pull back only when the viewer needs surrounding context.

For a manual ring: overlay an unfilled circle centered on the click point; animate its scale upward and opacity down over roughly 6–9 frames. Use the site's accent color and a thin stroke. Use a built-in click indicator if available to save time.

## Premium finish

- One background treatment, one type family, one accent color, one browser frame.
- Let titles animate in over roughly 0.3–0.5 seconds, then become still.
- Use mostly straight cuts. Reserve a larger transition for the brand reveal.
- Keep music underneath the voice. Start around 18–24 dB quieter than narration, then judge by listening; source levels differ.
- Use only a few purposeful sounds. Avoid a loud effect on every mouse movement.
- Check speech on laptop speakers and headphones. If you can measure loudness, roughly −16 to −14 LUFS integrated and true peak at or below −1 dBTP are useful web-video starting points, not submission requirements.
- Match captions to the final recorded words and leave room for the actual product controls. Check them at phone-sized playback.

## Suggested edit tracks

V1: background / underlying screen footage

V2: picture overlays, browser frame or occasional detail crop

V3: titles and captions

A1: voiceover

A2: music

A3: click, whoosh and confirmation effects

## Export and final check

Export H.264 MP4, 1920 × 1080, 30 fps, AAC audio unless rules require something else. A video bitrate around 12–20 Mbps is a reasonable starting point for readable UI at this size; lower it if the upload limit requires it. A 90-second video at 12 Mbps is approximately 135 MB before audio and overhead.

- [ ] Duration complies with the actual limit. If 90 seconds is a hard maximum, export about 89–89.5 seconds for margin.
- [ ] Opening explains the problem and the product appears early.
- [ ] Every asserted feature is demonstrated or explicitly labeled planned.
- [ ] Reading holds are long enough; text stays sharp after zooms.
- [ ] No placeholder cards, accidental notifications, black gaps or clipped audio.
- [ ] Brand name, captions, spelling and any destination URL are correct.
- [ ] Music/SFX rights permit the submission and its likely public display; record the source and license.
- [ ] Watch the exported file from beginning to end, not just the editor preview.
- [ ] Upload before the final buffer expires and verify the submitted link/file plays for its intended audience.
